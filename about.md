I'm Davis. I write software for a living and spend most of the rest of my time building things that didn't strictly need to exist.

This site is where both halves end up. [Projects](/projects) is the showroom — finished things, or things finished enough to show. The [blog](/blog) is the workshop journal, where the actual details live: what broke, what I tried, and what eventually worked.

## The software half

Most of my screen time lately goes to LLM research and tooling. I replicated and extended a paper on hallucination-associated neurons in Llama-3.1, and found that the circuits behind factual hallucinations and code hallucinations barely overlap at all. I ran a controlled experiment on whether prompting in Chinese actually saves tokens. It does, by about 10%, though just asking the model to be concise beats it six to one.

I also built a persistent agent daemon that lives across an Obsidian vault, a vector store, and a SQLite database, and messages me on Telegram when something needs attention.

The rest is small, sharp tools. A CLI that repairs broken STL meshes. An RSS ingestor that feeds a forecasting pipeline. A Polymarket trading bot whose 41-hour paper-trading post-mortem was considerably more interesting than its returns.

Mostly Python and TypeScript. I like a tool that does one thing and exits cleanly.

## The hardware half

Two Voron 3D printers: a 350mm 2.4 that started the whole rabbit hole, and a compact 120mm V0.2 that handles the smaller, faster prints. Both run Klipper. Between them they've printed most of the parts for everything else on this list.

Everything else tends to involve an ESP32 and a questionable idea. A control box that bridges a real morse paddle to a phone app, because tapping glass doesn't build muscle memory. An RFID cartridge player that turns streaming back into something you can hold. A pneumatic skeleton that lunges at people on Halloween from fifty feet away.

## Right now

Learning morse code, slowly. Chasing chamber temperature stability for ABS and ASA. Reading more about memory and attention in agent systems than is strictly reasonable.
